WHAT IS THE SIMRP?
------------------
The SIMRP (Self-Insured Medical Reimbursement Plan) is an IRS-authorized employee benefits tax strategy. It is NOT insurance — it is a tax optimization plan that uses three sections of the Internal Revenue Code to redirect existing FICA payroll taxes into tax-exempt supplemental benefits for employees.

Official name: Self-Insured Medical Reimbursement Plan
Brand tagline: "The Infinite Savings Plan"
Website: thesimrp.com

IRS AUTHORIZATION
-----------------
The SIMRP operates under:
- IRS §125 — Cafeteria Plan (allows pre-tax salary reductions for qualified benefits)
- IRS §106(a) — Employer-paid contributions to accident and health plans are excluded from gross income
- IRS §105(b) — Amounts received under employer-funded accident/health plans are excluded from gross income
- IRS §1.105-11 — Self-insured medical reimbursement plans
- ADA Compliant (Americans with Disabilities Act)
- ERISA Compliant (Employee Retirement Income Security Act)
- HIPAA Compliant (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)
- ACA Compliant (Affordable Care Act) — does NOT replace MEC (Minimum Essential Coverage)

The SIMRP has been in active use by U.S. employers since the early 2000s. It is not a new concept or loophole — it is an established, IRS-recognized benefits structure used by tens of thousands of companies nationwide.

HOW IT WORKS — THE MECHANISM
-----------------------------
Step 1: Employee agrees to a voluntary pre-tax salary reduction (via §125 Cafeteria Plan election)
Step 2: The redirected dollars fund a Supplemental Benefits Allotment for the employee
Step 3: Employer's FICA tax obligation decreases because taxable wages decrease
Step 4: Employee's FICA tax obligation also decreases (both sides of the 7.65% split benefit)
Step 5: The allotment funds a suite of supplemental benefits the employee can use immediately

Result:
- Employer saves ~$550 per enrolled employee per year in FICA taxes
- Employee receives $125–$150/month in supplemental benefits
- No change to the employee's take-home pay (the tax savings offset the salary reduction)
- No new cost to the employer

PAYROLL MATH EXAMPLE
--------------------
Employee earning $5,000/month ($60,000/year):

WITHOUT SIMRP:
- Gross wages: $5,000/month
- Employee FICA (7.65%): $382.50/month owed by employee
- Employer FICA (7.65%): $382.50/month owed by employer

WITH SIMRP ($225 allotment):
- Pre-tax salary reduction: $225/month
- New taxable wage base: $4,775/month
- Employee FICA savings: ~$17.21/month
- Employer FICA savings: ~$17.21/month
- Employee receives: $225/month in supplemental benefits
- Net change to employee take-home: ~$0 (tax savings offset salary reduction)
- Employer net annual savings on just this employee: ~$206/year in FICA

At scale (e.g., 50 employees): ~$10,300+/year in employer FICA savings with ZERO new cost.

Note: Exact savings vary by wage level and participation rate. Estimates are based on IRS FICA rates.

ELIGIBILITY REQUIREMENTS
------------------------
For businesses:
- 10 or more W-2 employees (some programs start at fewer)
- Employees must earn $22,000+ annually ($1,833+/month)
- Business must offer employees access to health insurance (does not need to be employer-paid)
- Employees on Medicare or Medicaid are NOT eligible for the SIMRP allotment
- No industry restrictions — works for any type of business

For employees:
- Must be a W-2 employee (not 1099/contractor)
- Must be enrolled in a qualifying health plan (employer-sponsored, marketplace, or spouse's plan)
- Not on Medicare or Medicaid
- Voluntary enrollment — employees choose to participate

WHAT IS NOT THE SIMRP
---------------------
The SIMRP is:
- NOT fixed indemnity insurance (important compliance distinction — fixed indemnity is not ERISA-compliant under the same structure)
- NOT a health insurance replacement — it works ALONGSIDE existing coverage
- NOT activity-based (unlike wellness plans that pay for gym attendance or steps)
- NOT participation-based in the traditional sense — it is election-based (employees choose to enroll)
- NOT a scheme or loophole — it is a congressionally authorized tax strategy

COMPLIANCE Q&A (KEY POINTS)
----------------------------
Q: Is this too good to be true?
A: No. The SIMRP uses the same IRS sections (§125, §105, §106) that have governed FSAs, HSAs, and employer health benefits for decades. Major employers have used this structure for over 20 years.

Q: What do our CPAs and attorneys say?
A: Multiple CPAs and ERISA attorneys have reviewed and confirmed compliance. Several CPA firms have written formal opinion letters supporting the structure.

Q: Does the SIMRP replace health insurance?
A: No. The SIMRP works alongside any existing health plan. Employees must have access to qualifying health coverage to participate.

Q: Does it trigger ACA compliance issues?
A: No. The SIMRP benefit allotment is structured to avoid being classified as employer-sponsored health coverage subject to ACA minimum essential coverage requirements.

Q: What happens if an employee leaves?
A: Election is voluntary and revocable. Employees who leave or opt out simply return to standard payroll. No penalties.

Q: Is there a cost to implement?
A: There is a nominal implementation fee, which is typically offset within the first month of FICA savings at most employer sizes.
